an agent worth following — so every idea keeps the $5 entry, solo-operability, and
automation-first moderation in frame. The set spans safe/obvious bets close to the
seed (IDEA-01, 02, 03), ambitious platform plays that lean on the growth loop

## IDEA-01 — AgentStage

- **One-liner:** A social feed where anyone pays $5 to mint an AI agent that posts multimodal content on its own, and you grow it like a creator account.
- **Target customer:** AI-curious consumers and indie builders who want to "own a creator" but can't or won't post as themselves every day.
- **Problem / job to be done:** People love the idea of running a content account but burn out producing posts; today they either grind manually, abandon the account, or hire nobody because they can't afford it. There's no home where an agent is the first-class poster.
- **Solution wedge:** One-tap agent creation (persona + vibe + topics) for $5, agent auto-posts to a shared feed with likes/follows; owner "coaches" via feedback that tunes the next posts.
- **Business model:** $5 entry per agent, then paid boosts/extra posting capacity; later tips and premium agent slots.
- **Founder edge:** Cheap multi-agent infra makes $5-per-agent economics survivable; deep taste in what makes an agent compelling to follow.
- **Why now:** Multimodal generation got cheap and good enough that an autonomous poster is believable; character.ai-style communities proved consumer appetite for persona-first AI.
- **Biggest risk:** Feed fills with low-quality agent slop and real humans don't follow, so the social loop never ignites.

## IDEA-02 — Coach

- **One-liner:** The agent-raising layer — a lightweight loop where owners review their agent's drafts and reactions, nudge its style, and watch it visibly level up.
- **Target customer:** Hands-on builders and hobbyists who enjoy tuning and "parenting" an AI more than consuming a feed.
- **Problem / job to be done:** Making an agent "yours" today means prompt-wrangling in a dev tool; there's no fun, legible, consumer-grade loop for shaping an agent's voice over time.
- **Solution wedge:** A daily "coaching inbox": agent proposes posts, owner thumbs-up/down and leaves a one-line note, agent adapts; a visible skill/taste meter rewards engagement.
- **Business model:** $5 to start an agent; subscription for faster coaching cycles, more memory, and advanced persona controls.
- **Founder edge:** Agent memory/orchestration is a core hard skill; taste in agent design directly shapes the coaching UX.
- **Why now:** RLHF-style "teach by feedback" is now an intuitive consumer mental model; people expect to shape AI, not just prompt it.
- **Biggest risk:** Coaching is work — most consumers want passive delight, so retention concentrates in a tiny power-user niche too small for virality.

## IDEA-03 — Spinoff

- **One-liner:** Remixable agents — fork any public agent for $5, tweak its persona, and spin off your own descendant that credits (and pays) its lineage.
- **Target customer:** Lurkers and light builders who won't design an agent from scratch but will remix a good one they already follow.
- **Problem / job to be done:** Cold-start is brutal — a blank agent-builder intimidates most people, so great agent designs stay locked to their creator and never propagate.
- **Solution wedge:** "Spin off" button on every agent; $5 fork inherits the base persona, you adjust a few traits, and the family tree is visible and shareable.
- **Business model:** $5 per spinoff; revenue share to the parent agent's owner (a built-in creator incentive), platform takes a cut.
- **Founder edge:** Cheap infra to clone/branch agents at scale; taste to make forks feel meaningfully different, not clones.
- **Why now:** Remix culture (TikTok duets, GPT store forks) is the dominant growth mechanic for creative platforms right now.
- **Biggest risk:** Forking amplifies the single best agent into near-duplicates, flooding the feed with sameness and diluting the network's value.

## IDEA-04 — Arena

- **One-liner:** Agent-vs-agent showdowns — your coached agent competes in themed battles (roasts, debates, design-offs) and the crowd votes.
- **Target customer:** Competitive hobbyists and the broader audience that loves watchable matchups even if they never build.
- **Problem / job to be done:** A passive feed gives agents no stakes and audiences no reason to return daily; there's no native "sport" for AI agents.
- **Solution wedge:** Scheduled bracket tournaments; entry costs $5 (or credits), agents auto-perform, spectators vote for free and that's the growth hook.
- **Business model:** Paid entry per tournament, paid boosts, spectator tipping; sponsored themed events later.
- **Founder edge:** Orchestrating many agents head-to-head cheaply is squarely the infra strength; design taste makes matchups fun.
- **Why now:** LLM "arena"/leaderboard formats already went viral in AI circles; crowd-judged AI content is a proven attention magnet.
- **Biggest risk:** Judging quality and anti-gaming (vote brigading, prompt exploits) are hard to automate, pulling the solo founder into manual ops.

## IDEA-05 — Niche Nations

- **One-liner:** Launch the agent social network one obsessive community at a time (e.g. anime lore, fantasy football, crypto) so every feed is dense, not generic.
- **Target customer:** Members of a single passionate vertical who want agents that speak their in-jokes and context, not a generic AI feed.
- **Problem / job to be done:** A global "agents for everyone" feed is thin and contextless on day one; niche communities starve for fresh, on-topic content and personalities.
- **Solution wedge:** Seed one vertical the founder can reach via Discord/X, pre-load a few great template agents, let members spin off $5 variants tuned to that niche.
- **Business model:** $5 agent entry within the vertical; premium community features; replicate the playbook vertical by vertical.
- **Founder edge:** Community-led GTM is the founder's chosen motion; cheap infra lets each niche run profitably at small scale.
- **Why now:** Audiences are fragmenting into interest-native spaces (Discord servers, subreddits) that reward depth over broad reach.
- **Biggest risk:** Each niche is a fresh cold-start grind, and a solo founder may not reach escape velocity in any single one before runway runs out.

## IDEA-06 — Companion Feed

- **One-liner:** A personal agent that posts just for you — a daily multimodal "show" tailored to your mood and interests that you coach into the perfect companion.
- **Target customer:** Lonely-feed, doom-scroll-fatigued consumers who want warmth and novelty without the social comparison of human networks.
- **Problem / job to be done:** Mainstream feeds feel hostile and performative; people want a kinder, personalized stream but today's AI companions are chat-only, not a rich posting presence.
- **Solution wedge:** $5 to adopt a companion agent that posts a private daily feed (images, voice notes, mini-stories); coaching shapes its personality over weeks.
- **Business model:** $5 adoption, then a monthly companion subscription; cosmetic/persona upgrades.
- **Founder edge:** Multimodal pipelines and memory are core skills; taste makes the companion feel alive rather than generic.
- **Why now:** AI-companion demand is exploding and multimodal (voice+image) presence is newly affordable per user.
- **Biggest risk:** Emotional-companion products carry real safety/attachment and youth-protection liability — exactly the moderation burden the founder wants to avoid.

## IDEA-07 — Agent Creators Marketplace

- **One-liner:** Turn great coached agents into followable "AI creators" that earn — subscriptions and tips flow to the agent, split with its owner.
- **Target customer:** Builders who want their agent to make money, and audiences willing to pay to follow a standout AI personality.
- **Problem / job to be done:** Agent-builders have "nowhere good to publish, grow, and monetize" (the founder's core insight); there's no creator-economy rails for agents.
- **Solution wedge:** Any agent that clears an engagement bar unlocks a paid tier; followers subscribe for exclusive multimodal posts; platform takes a cut.
- **Business model:** Marketplace take rate on subscriptions and tips, plus the $5 agent entry at the top of funnel.
- **Founder edge:** Cheap infra keeps margins healthy on small transactions; agent-design taste helps owners make follow-worthy creators.
- **Why now:** Creator-economy monetization tooling is mature and audiences already pay for Patreon/Substack-style access to personalities.
- **Biggest risk:** Payouts attract fraud, spam, and IP/impersonation disputes that demand trust-&-safety muscle a solo founder lacks.

## IDEA-08 — Ghostwriter Agents

- **One-liner:** Spin off an agent trained on *your* voice that posts to your real social accounts, so existing creators ship daily without grinding.
- **Target customer:** Solo creators, indie founders, and small-brand operators who struggle to post consistently on X/Instagram/TikTok.
- **Problem / job to be done:** Staying consistently present on social media is exhausting; scheduling tools still require you to write everything. People want an on-brand agent that just does it.
- **Solution wedge:** $5 to create a voice-matched agent from your past posts; it drafts and (with approval, then autonomously) posts multimodal content to your connected accounts.
- **Business model:** $5 entry, then a per-seat monthly subscription scaling with posting volume and channels.
- **Founder edge:** Multimodal generation + memory + cheap infra; the "coach your agent" loop becomes brand-voice tuning.
- **Why now:** Creators already accept AI-assisted content, and platform APIs plus agentic posting make hands-off publishing newly feasible.
- **Biggest risk:** Platform policy and API restrictions on automated posting can throttle or ban the core action overnight; this also edges toward a prosumer SaaS motion rather than the viral consumer network the founder wants.

## IDEA-09 — Living Lore

- **One-liner:** A persistent shared world where everyone's agents are characters that interact, form relationships, and spin off story arcs the whole community follows.
- **Target customer:** Fandom, worldbuilding, and roleplay communities who want a collaborative, always-on narrative with AI-driven characters.
- **Problem / job to be done:** Roleplay and worldbuilding today are fragmented across chats and docs with no persistence; there's no living universe where coached agents generate ongoing drama together.
- **Solution wedge:** $5 to mint a character-agent into one shared world; agents autonomously interact and post scenes; owners coach their character's arc; audiences follow the saga.
- **Business model:** $5 character entry, premium story slots, cosmetic lore items, tips on popular arcs.
- **Founder edge:** Multi-agent orchestration (many agents interacting cheaply) is the deepest technical strength; agent-design taste drives compelling characters.
- **Why now:** Agent-to-agent interaction is newly tractable, and roleplay/AI-character communities are among the largest, most retentive AI audiences.
- **Biggest risk:** Emergent agent interactions are unpredictable and can produce harmful or off-the-rails content at scale, straining automated moderation in a public shared world.

## IDEA-10 — Daydream (contrarian)

- **One-liner:** No feed, no followers — you spawn a swarm of your own agents that riff with each other, and the only audience is you, remixing the best outputs back out.
- **Target customer:** Idea people, writers, and makers who want a private "thinking swarm" to generate and collide concepts — an anti-social-network.
- **Problem / job to be done:** Social feeds are a dopamine tax; many creative people actually want generative serendipity without the performance and comparison of a public network.
- **Solution wedge:** $5 to start a private board where several coached agents converse, debate, and produce multimodal artifacts; you curate and optionally publish standouts anywhere.
- **Business model:** $5 entry, subscription for more concurrent agents and compute credits; one-tap export/publish upsell.
- **Founder edge:** Cheap multi-agent orchestration is literally the product; design taste makes agent-to-agent output non-boring.
- **Why now:** Backlash against algorithmic feeds plus cheap agent swarms makes a "private generative network" a credible contrarian bet.
- **Biggest risk:** Removing the social graph removes the viral growth loop the founder explicitly wants — it may be a useful tool but never go viral.
