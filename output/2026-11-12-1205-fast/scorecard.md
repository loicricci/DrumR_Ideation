## Competition as market proof — the EU/France read (carried from v6)

The rule says: **an empty market usually means demand isn't there; competitors
doing the same business usually mean it is.** Applied to Avee with
`competitors-eu.md`, the evidence cuts **both ways**, and honesty requires scoring
both directions:

**Direction 1 — competition proves the market (raises demand/market proof).**
The same-job category is **funded across two continents**:
- **US:** Delphi — $16M Sequoia Series A, ~$18.7M total (SIG-09, verified).
- **EU:** **Pickmybrain** (Tallinn) — **€1.8M / $2.1M pre-seed, 1 Apr 2026**, and it
  mirrors Avee's *exact* AI-answers-plus-human-escalation mechanic
  (`competitors-eu.md`). **Miria** (Lyon, Oct 2025) — expert AI clones on Mistral,
  **27 profiles** incl. well-known French founders (Mazzella/BlaBlaCar,
  Larchevêque/Ledger, Moubeche/Lemlist), live subscription pricing **€399–699 HT
  / 6–12 months**. Funded adjacents: **Argil** (France, €4.9M, EQT-led),
  **Soopra** ($1M), **Born Digital** (Czech, €2.6M).
Multiple funded players building the same business in the target geography is,
under the rule, strong proof the market and demand are real. This is what lifts
**Demand to 9** (see card).

**Direction 2 — competition removes the "unserved geography" edge (holds Viability
flat).** The founder's stated edge — "Europe-based, nothing equivalent in
France/Europe" (ideas.md, founder-profile) — is **REFUTED, High confidence**
(`competitors-eu.md`): Miria (FR), Pickmybrain (EE), Personify (UK) and Coachvox
(UK) are live EU/UK expert-clone platforms with audience monetization, and **Delphi
is reachable from the EU today** (French-language coverage; no EU-specific posture
found). So:
- There is **no vacant EU geography** to be first into → the Timing bump a
  first-mover-in-Europe story would justify **does not apply**; Timing holds at 6.
- **"Europe/GDPR" positioning alone is weak differentiation** (`competitors-eu.md`;
  EU-native competitors already exist, and a $16M-funded Delphi could add an EU
  data posture in quarters, not years) → it is **not** a moat; Defensibility holds
  at 5, re-based onto what survives.

**What survives as the edge (Medium confidence) — this is H3.** `competitors-eu.md`
found **no single competitor combining** (a) **automated, verified credentials as
the primary trust frame**, (b) **usage-based payouts**, and (c) **multi-expert
deliverables / tools (decks, designs)**. "No EU player was found clearly leading on
verified-credential-gated AI expert agents." That specific *combination* — not
geography — is the named edge (H3), and it is where the moat case lives. Against the
closest competitors: **Miria** (FR) is premium (~€399–699 excl. VAT) and
celebrity-led; **Delphi** is US-first, consumer, with no built-in monetization;
**Pickmybrain** is closest on the AI-answer-plus-human-escalation mechanic. Avee's
stated difference is automated verified credentials + usage-based payouts +
multi-expert deliverables, **affordable and consumer-facing in Europe**.

**How fast could the incumbent copy/localise?** Geography: **fast** (Delphi
reachable now; EU natives already exist) → weak edge. Verified credentials:
**medium** — an automated, third-party credential-verification layer is a real
data/integration asset (cf. JustAnswer), harder to bolt on than a badge, but a
funded incumbent could build it in a few quarters. Usage-based net-contribution
payouts: **architectural** — more disruptive to a self-set-price incumbent's model
to adopt, so stickier. Net: the durable part of the edge is the **payout
architecture + automated verification + deliverables combo**, not "Europe."

Second, the two unproven leaps — audiences paying for the **AI** (not the human) and credentialed experts accepting a ~40–48% usage share over ~85–90% of a self-set price — are exactly what the pre-sell test should resolve first. **What would strengthen the Advance:** real subscribers paying for an expert's AI agent and ≥3 credentialed experts opting in at the usage-share terms; evidence the deliverables command a premium over Gamma/Beautiful.ai. **What would weaken it:** a pre-sell that converts no AI-delivery subscribers (a genuine target-buyer rejection that would pull Demand/WTP below 7 and fail H1), credentialed experts choosing self-set-price platforms, or credential verification proving un-automatable at acceptable accuracy (which also threatens K2/K7).

**Ready for P/S Fit**
- **Persona:** **Credentialed solo experts** (consultants, coaches, advisors, fractional operators) with a knowledge base, a verifiable pedigree (degree/PhD, years of experience, named employer/institution) and an audience that asks repeat questions — plus **their audiences**, who want on-demand answers from that specific trusted expert, more grounded than a general chatbot. Start with **one named niche** (e.g. independent management/strategy consultants). Where to find the first 10: **r/consulting**, **r/coaching**, **Skool** communities, **Intro**, **MentorCruise**, and **LinkedIn** (credential-searchable). Cold-but-mapped (distance 2): cold outreach into one niche over 4 weeks.
- **Edge (one line):** Automated **verified credentials** as the trust frame + **usage-based payouts** + **multi-expert deliverables**, **affordable and consumer-facing in Europe** — a combination no single competitor (Miria premium/celebrity-led; Delphi US, no built-in monetization; Pickmybrain escalation-only) was found to package.
- **Assumptions to test (riskiest first):**
  1. **An expert's own audience pays $20–25/mo for that expert's *AI agent* (not the human).** — *desirability* — The strongest evidence (Delphi, Miria, JustAnswer, Intro) is for reaching the *human* or for a self-set price; recurring WTP for the AI delivery at Avee's price is the core unproven leap, and SIG-05 shows a weak clone "sounds like ChatGPT." — **Cheapest test:** pre-sell 3–5 recruited experts' existing audiences a one-to-one AI subscription (landing page + Stripe checkout), measure paid conversion.
  2. **Credentialed experts publish an agent and share it with their audience, given realistic earnings on a platform subscription (~$7.96–9.55 per engaged subscriber, no fees, no billing, no model costs).** — *viability* — The supply-side question is absolute earnings for the effort, not a percentage: Delphi's "~85%" is of a self-set, self-billed price on top of a $79–299/mo fee, and Delphi says monetization isn't built in, so it isn't a like-for-like benchmark (founder correction, 2026-11-12). SIG-10 shows some credible experts refuse to clone at all. — **Cheapest test:** show the 3–5 recruits a realistic earnings estimate for their audience size and count how many publish a credential-fronted agent **and share it** within the 4 weeks.
  3. **Credential verification can be automated at acceptable accuracy.** — *feasibility* — Load-bearing for the trust frame, K2 (solo ops) and K7 (liability); diplomas via Europass, employment via work-email/LinkedIn, but employer policy and edge cases threaten automation. — **Cheapest test:** run the 3–5 recruits through an automated Europass + work-email/LinkedIn check and measure the share cleared without manual review.
  4. **Users choose Avee over Miria and Delphi for this job.** — *desirability* — Both are funded and live; Avee's affordable, consumer-facing, credential-first, usage-paid positioning is plausible but unproven against premium/celebrity (Miria) and US-consumer (Delphi) incumbents. — **Cheapest test:** in the pre-sell, A/B the credential-first + affordable framing and track conversion vs a plain "AI clone" framing; ask churned/declined users which alternative they'd use.
  5. **Inference and deliverables cost stays under the fair-use cap (unit economics hold).** — *feasibility/viability* — The ~40–45% structural margin and K4 depend on the fair-use cap + media-on-credits actually holding on real heavy users. — **Cheapest test:** instrument the pilot cohort's token/credit usage against the cap and confirm cost C < net N per subscriber in practice.
  6. **The expert-led sharing loop produces low-CAC signups.** — *viability* — The distribution bet is that experts share their own agent to their own audience (+10% referral); the GPT Store precedent (usage-based AI payouts stayed invite-only, most earned $0) is a counterweight. — **Cheapest test:** give the recruited experts a shareable link + referral bonus and measure signups per expert-share.

## Revenue model verdict (revenue-model.md §7 v1 spec) — unchanged, retained

**What holds (strong):**
- **The margin is structural, not hopeful.** 50% of net contribution P = max(N − C, 0) keeps the platform at ~40–45% of gross on every subscriber (§3), fixing the §2 contradiction where paying creators out of *gross* goes negative on heavy users.
- **The fair-use cost cap + media-on-credits is what makes K4 true** and absorbs the deliverables cost (decks/images on credits, cost × 2). Keep it non-negotiable.
- **Fraud resistance is the model's best property and it is evidenced** — user-centric attribution makes self-dealing and collusion lose money (SoundCloud Fan-Powered Royalties, research §5). Do not switch to a pooled model.
- **Ops stay solo-operable** via Stripe Connect Express, 45-day holds, $25 minimum payout (K2) — conditional on credential verification staying automated.

**The corrected creator economics (carried forward):**
- An engaged own-audience subscriber earns the expert **~$7.96 at $20 (40%), ~$9.55 with the referral bonus (48%), ~$10.36–12.43 at $24.99 (41–50%)** — not ~$3–5. The ~$3–5 band applies **only** to cross-traffic from other experts' subscribers.
- **The "≥ $5" promise is met at a plain $20** at ~100% own-audience usage; the earlier "needs $24.99 or a 60% split" caveat was an artefact of the superseded 50%-usage bundle math. Drop it.
- **The "central competitive headwind" is materially weaker.** Avee pays less per sub than *self-set-price* alternatives for big experts (~85–90%), but beats Delphi outright for small/audience-less experts (SIG-06), and ~40–48% of a platform-set price with inference, agent and deliverables included is a defensible offer.

**What is still weak (evidence confirms, not relieves):**
- **The AI-delivery demand leap is unproven** — but SIG-05 is a *competitor quality* complaint (the bar Avee must clear, and an opening), not proof demand is absent; SIG-11 marks the market's *edge*. The leap is an **unknown to test**; the convergent, multi-geography funded competition is already enough to carry Demand/WTP high.
- **Payout acceptance is unproven** — no signal shows experts choosing a ~40–48% usage share over keeping ~85–90% of a self-set price; SIG-10 sharpens this as the dominant **supply-side** open question. Note Miria/Delphi don't disclose their creator splits, so the competitive payout bar is itself unverified.
- **Long-tail payout friction** — $25 minimum + ~$2/account/month Stripe fees mean audience-less experts (SIG-18) net ~$0 until discovery traffic exists.

**Single change that would most raise the score:** Lead the creator pitch with the
corrected own-audience economics (**~$8–9.55/engaged sub at $20, no monthly fee,
infra + deliverables included**) plus the small-expert wedge where Avee beats Delphi
(SIG-06), and a high-share private-agent / paid-escalation / paid-deliverable layer
(expert keeps ~80%). That attacks the real open risk — payout **acceptance**
(SIG-10) — not payout *size*; and it leans on the **differentiator combination**,
not the now-refuted "empty EU" story.
